What if a ship's structure could do more than hold it together?

A 12-metre sphere. 120 tubes. Two families of 60.

ARÈS-7 is a 12 m sphere made of 120 tubes. The geometry sorts them by itself into two families of 60: the short ones are designed as plasma channels, the long ones as the load-bearing structure. In the concept, plasma flows through all 120 and makes them glow. Guiding principle: no part should exist for a single function.

A concept at the study stage, dated by an e-Soleau deposit with INPI, the French patent office (6 July 2026: proof of priority, not a patent). It cannot be built today; the dossier states exactly what is missing.

The idea in 3 points

① A split imposed by geometry

A frequency-2 geodesic sphere (a subdivided icosahedron) has 42 nodes and 120 edges, which split exactly into 60 radial edges of 3.28 m and 60 inner edges of 3.71 m. ARÈS-7 gives each family a function. 🟢 verified

② One part, six functions

Each edge is designed to be at once structure, plasma channel, shielding, debris protection, light source and a piezoelectric modulation element. In the concept, the flowing plasma would create the magnetic field: a protective bubble against radiation, then the ejection of plasmoids for thrust. 🔵 not demonstrated

③ Bright light, no halo

The flowing helium plasma would make all 120 edges glow: the structure lights the ship. In a vacuum nothing scatters light: no halo, just sharp lines. 🟢 no halo Target colour: a slightly warm white, still to be measured. 🔵 to be demonstrated

What is missing (and where we need you)

  1. The main lock: power density. The goal is total freedom of movement; to reach it, power density must be multiplied by 5,000 to 30,000. 🔵
  2. Compact fusion of a few MW: projects exist, none is ready yet. 🔵
  3. The next physical proof: a 1:10 model of the contactless magnetic bearing (from about €350). It is the most concrete building block. 🟡 to be built
